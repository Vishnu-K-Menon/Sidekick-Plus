# Research briefing

## When to use

Use this skill when the user wants findings written down: a briefing, a comparison of options, or a recommendation. The deliverable is one markdown file, short enough to read in two minutes.

## Research

- Search first, then read the two or three best pages in full rather than trusting search snippets
- Prefer primary sources: the maker's own page, an official body, or a named publication
- Note the address of every page a fact came from as you go
- If two sources disagree on a number, say so and give both

## Structure of the file

1. A title that states the question
2. **Bottom line**: one or two sentences with the answer, before anything else
3. One short section per option or topic, each with three to five bullet points and concrete numbers
4. A comparison table when there are two or more options
5. **Recommendation**: the decision, the runner-up, and the one condition that would change it
6. **Sources**: a numbered list of links, each cited at least once in the text as [1], [2]

## House style

- Plain words and short sentences; no filler opening or closing
- Every number has a unit and a source
- Say what you could not find rather than leaving it out
